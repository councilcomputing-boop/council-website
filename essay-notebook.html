An AI writing coach that sounds like the student, not like AI.

Council Computing built Essay Notebook, a writing coach for students. They paste an essay prompt, plan it with the coach, then write it one paragraph at a time. The coach drafts each paragraph in the student's own voice, which it learns from their writing, and the student approves every word.

The problem

AI writing tools do the work for students, badly

Most chatbots hand back a whole essay at once. It doesn't sound like the student, and they don't learn anything from it.

It sounds like a robot wrote it

Generic AI prose has a voice teachers recognize right away, and it reads nothing like the student's other work.

It makes things up

Chatbots invent quotes, statistics, and personal stories that never happened, then present them as fact.

What we built

A coach that keeps the student in charge

Every feature is built around one idea: the student controls every word, and the result should sound like them.

Learns the student's voice

Students paste past writing, answer three short prompts, or talk it through. Code then measures their real sentence length, word choice, and habits, so the style is grounded in numbers instead of guesses.

Plan first

The coach proposes a title, thesis, and one point per paragraph. The student edits, reorders, or asks for a different take before any writing starts.

One paragraph at a time

Each paragraph can be approved, edited, redone, or fixed line by line with a highlighter. The student's edits teach the coach their style as they go.

Never invents facts

Where it needs something only the student knows, it leaves a [yellow spot] for them to fill in instead of making up a quote or a memory.

A finished, formatted paper

MLA, APA, or Chicago formatting, then download as PDF or Word. Rubric check, peer review, and final touches catch what's left.

A writing record

Every draft, edit, and answer is kept, and the student can share a read-only link with a teacher to show how the essay came together.

The real thing

Simple enough for a phone

Built mobile-first, with a calm notebook look so it feels like writing, not like a chatbot.

How it works

From prompt to finished paper

1

Find your voice

Share past writing or answer a few short prompts so the coach learns how you write.

2

Plan the essay

Paste the prompt and rubric, then shape the thesis and outline together.

3

Write paragraph by paragraph

Answer the coach's questions, then approve, edit, or redo each draft.

4

Finish and polish

Get the formatted paper, check it against the rubric, and download it.
